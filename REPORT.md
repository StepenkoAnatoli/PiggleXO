# Businesses that can operate while the owner works

**Research date: 12 November 2026. Recommendation: run one small demand experiment; do not build or launch a portfolio.** The best justified first test is an original, narrowly scoped **craft-market closeout and event-comparison template**, sold once for a hypothesized **$29**, with **Pinterest as its sole initial acquisition channel**. First compare the proposed simpler workflow with free and $19 alternatives; stop if it offers no useful difference. No examined business is proven to deliver reliable profit within 60 owner minutes/week from zero audience. A measured **60–120 minute mature-week tier**, plus initial laptop work and occasional incidents, is the smallest reasonable relaxation to test.

Confidence is **moderate in the operational preference for a static, tested product; low in demand for this particular offer, its premium price and its acquisition economics**. The recommendation minimizes cash exposure and urgent fulfillment dependencies. It does not establish an underserved market: existing competitors already offer much of the functionality. A failed cheap test is a useful outcome.

The customer-side signal is a maker asking to compare inventory by market while already using an annual spreadsheet. Replies show workable paper, Excel and inventory-software substitutes. A current vendor offers similar event comparison for $19 and a broader $29 bundle. These sources justify testing a specific task while challenging the proposed price; they do not prove profitable repeatability. [Original customer discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/CraftFairs/comments/1givbp2/tracking_inventory/), [close competitor](https://plainsheetstudio.com/makers/templates/craft-fair-tracker/).

A published price is supply evidence, not proof of sales. Forum testimony is operator-reported, not audited fact.

## Assumptions and dependencies

Solo owner with a job, no audience, brand, list or privileged distribution; USD scenarios at $250/$1,000/$5,000 capital; routine supervision by phone; preferred <=60 minutes/week, <=120 when justified. Up to ten workday hours unavailable is a planning assumption, not a confirmed schedule. Setup and incident laptop work are allowed but counted separately. No country, payment eligibility, professional expertise, employer permission or willingness to appear on camera is inferred from the device or repository owner.

Before a paid pilot, confirm seller and customer geography, provider eligibility/product classification, actual refund funding path, local registration/accounting needs, rights to original assets and files, subject competence, and employer restrictions. These are dependent launch questions; they did not block public research or local calculations. The report does not assume a merchant-of-record service removes the owner's income tax or all business obligations. Country-specific legal/tax conclusions were not attempted.

The search covered content/affiliate/sponsorship, digital products/templates/media/newsletters/directories, SaaS/extensions/APIs/devtools, data monitoring/document workflows, standardized services/dropservicing, POD/dropshipping/low inventory, creator and AI infrastructure, and OSS-based products. Customer complaints and manual workarounds were searched without “AI.” English coverage dominates; French commerce guidance, a Portuguese workflow example, Korean/Japanese competitor materials and a limited German/Spanish craft-market search supplied cross-checks, not evidence of the owner's local demand. A German vendor's fictional AI-assisted story was excluded from customer-demand evidence. [Coverage and access log](research/coverage-and-checks.md).

## Ranking after feasibility checks

The hard screen rejects core workflows requiring unavailable rights, prohibited private publishing, production access without consent, urgent workday responses or uncapped custom revisions. Unknown but cheaply testable facts remain conditional. No weighted average can rescue a known blocker. Rankings are ordinal judgments for this owner, not precise scores or success probabilities.

| Rank / role | Offer and repeat need | Decision | Strongest supported reason / strongest rejection case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1; strongest overall test, social route and smallest-budget option | **F3** $29 one-time craft-event closeout template; repeated customer use, new sales needed each month | **Advance to validation**, not launch | Delivery and support can pause acquisition without a live integration. Free/$19 substitutes and no audience make paid demand/acquisition the central risk. |
| 2; potential larger ticket | **F4** $149/month fixed operational report from a bounded customer-owned export | **Conditional** | Recurring manual work exists and standardization is possible. Sales, mapping, month-end peaks and revisions can turn it into a job. |
| 3; overlooked commercial adaptation | **F2** $79/month weekly source-linked commercial-change brief for one shared niche | **Conditional** | A short human relevance decision can add value. Cheap monitors already summarize changes; source maintenance and editorial work may exceed the budget. |
| 4; demoted technical experiment | **F1** $39/month supplier-specific preflight/report, after a $29 one-off service test | **Conditional narrow workflow; reject generic checker launch** | Rules can be deterministic and nonurgent. Multiple local/free comparison tools remove the apparent gap; phone production controls remain unbuilt. |

The best **original/overlooked technical experiment** is I03: a narrow acceptance check for AI-produced localization values submitted through an API path whose documentation says project QA is not applied. It is a real, specific contract to test, but not a proven business. It requires relevant technical competence and customers using that path; the vendor could fix it cheaply. It does not displace the selected F3 demand test. [I03 and closest alternatives](research/inventions.md).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F3 | Low–moderate task evidence; low willingness-to-pay evidence | Low | Low empirical confidence; arithmetic reproducible | Established file delivery; actual product/phone untested | Low, modeled ~80 min/week |
| F4 | Moderate adjacent task evidence; exact budget unknown | Low | Low; price/customization assumptions dominate | Ordinary reporting feasible; customer schemas untested | Low; month-end peaks material |

What could reverse the ranking: verified relevant expertise/distribution, buyers already requesting a particular report, a permissioned recurring data source with demonstrated value, unacceptable spreadsheet support, inability to receive/refund payments, or real measurements showing that content acquisition needs several hours/week. If <=60 minutes is absolute, keep a smaller/slower one-product experiment or reject the opportunity; do not assume 80-minute economics survive a 25% work reduction.

## Social media is six different businesses

| Route | Payer / economic event | Fit for this owner |
|---|---|---|
| A: original media payouts | Platform pays for eligible monetized views after program conditions | Low initial fit: eligibility, production and volatile reach; total views are not eligible paid views |
| B: affiliate/social commerce | Merchant pays for an attributable qualifying purchase, subject to reversals/delays | Conditional; genuine demonstrations, current terms and useful purchase intent matter |
| C: own product | Customer buys a tested file/tool/subscription | Best initial social test; first sale need not await payout eligibility; conversion still unknown |
| D: B2B/service acquisition | Client pays for a scoped outcome | Higher ticket, but inquiries, intake and revisions consume time |
| E: sponsor/assets/licensing | Brand/customer licenses work or commissions a batch | Original rights and review matter; face/voice production is optional only for suitable formats |
| F: tools sold to creators | Creator pays to solve their workflow | Different buyer; free Passionfroot and established schedulers are strong substitutes |

The appendix separately checks YouTube long-form/Shorts, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest and LinkedIn. Native Pinterest scheduling is a documented phone-capable route for standard business-account Pins, with a ten-Pin queue and 30-day horizon as read; the account itself was not tested. TikTok's Direct Post rules prohibit the proposed private/internal uploader utility and require user choices/consent. API existence is not permission for unattended publishing. Meta access gaps are left open; no universal Facebook/Instagram view payout is assumed. [Pinterest scheduler](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/schedule-pins), [TikTok guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-sharing-guidelines).

The defined six-operator sample includes a small new storefront, a first-sale claim, a zero-sale Pinterest start, and a creator leaving a growing YouTube channel because of work/time pressure. It is a convenience sample read once, not analytics or a representative return distribution. Reported revenue is unaudited and never fed into the financial forecasts. No video contents/timestamps were inferred from titles. [Sample method, periods, URLs and limitations](research/social-media.md).

For the chosen offer, use two original faceless static Pins/week and one worked example. No music/likeness or borrowed footage is needed. Voice or on-camera work adds production time without established conversion benefit. Keep a domain/original files and consented customer export where permitted; followers are not an owned mailing list. A sale does not automatically authorize marketing email. Do not add affiliate/sponsor income to template revenue without a separate measured funnel.

## Financial results and the workload they require

All figures below are **illustrative conditional monthly USD scenarios**, not expected earnings. Sales tax is excluded from revenue. Fees, refund/chargeback allowances, delivery/retries, acquisition, fixed tools/admin/hosting and owner time are included. Owner labor is valued at $30/hour; operating profit is before owner pay and income tax, economic profit after owner labor. The complete conservative/base/optimistic inputs, cash timing and combined stress cases are [reproducible here](models/assumptions.md).

| Base case | Volume / price | Gross revenue | Operating profit | Economic profit | Owner minutes/week |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| F3 template | 20 orders × $29 | $580 | $329 | $155 | 80 |
| F4 monthly report | 5 accounts × $149 | $745 | $499 | $353 | 67 |
| F2 watchlist | 12 accounts × $79 | $948 | $608 | $436 | 79 |
| F1 preflight | 25 accounts × $39 | $975 | $697 | $564 | 61 before acquisition-channel sensitivity; ~64 with modeled conversations |

F1's higher calculated margin does not make it the strongest recommendation: neither its differentiating value nor low-support/self-service acquisition has been shown. Initial acquisition cash to create the base books is $875/$840/$450 for F1/F2/F4 respectively at assumed CACs, plus owner's launch time; steady-state figures include replacement acquisition only. F3 must win 20 new orders every month. Its $6 cash CAC is particularly fragile: $0.50 clicks at 2% conversion imply $25 CAC and negative unit margin. Organic acquisition still consumes the production/analysis time in the model.

At 2% qualified visit-to-purchase conversion, F3's $1,000 operating target requires 60 monthly orders and 3,000 qualified visits, roughly 91 owner minutes/week under fixed production assumptions. At 1% content-to-site click-through this is 300,000 relevant impressions; no such reach has been established. $5,000 requires 292 orders and ~150 modeled minutes before scale effects, beyond the design cap. The recurring alternatives' $5,000 targets also exceed capacity/workload: F1 167 accounts/~154 baseline minutes, F2 92/~342, F4 47/~256. These algebraic targets are requirements, not growth projections. [All scenarios, break-even and targets](models/results.md).

The fee check changed the calculations: Gumroad's direct fee help adds card processing to the headline rate, so the model uses 12.9% + $0.80 for direct card sales. Payment routes can differ. Lemon Squeezy's lower headline includes add-ons and prohibits services; a manual report cannot simply be routed through it as software. Both payout timing and accessible refund funds matter. [Gumroad fee detail](https://gumroad.com/help/article/66-gumroads-fees), [Lemon Squeezy fee detail](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees), [product restrictions](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products).

For the selected experiment, $250 permits at most $150 nonreserve spend and $100 refund/payout liquidity; $1,000 permits $250 spend/$150 liquidity and leaves $600 untouched; $5,000 permits $300 spend/$200 liquidity and leaves $4,500 untouched. These ceilings do not authorize spending. Stage 0 should cost $0 cash if possible. Plan two months with no revenue and no owner withdrawals. The bank reserve is not automatically a processor refund balance. [Selected budgets and cash gates](plans/validation.md).

## Implementation, reuse and supervision

For F3, build original formulas and a worked example only after the mockup comparison suggests a useful difference. Ordinary spreadsheet software and a supported download storefront are enough. AI can draft instructions, generate synthetic edge cases and propose content; a person must check calculations, usefulness, rights and claims. Generative AI is optional to delivery. Do not force model calls into a deterministic task.

F1 has an original offline Python prototype with explicit synthetic schema limits, local state and quiet repeated findings. It makes no external calls, never imports a catalog, and never claims Shopify compatibility. It demonstrates local parsing/deduplication, not the proposed production service or a business. The selected F3 product has **not** been built or tested; that is intentionally gated on differentiation. [Prototype scope/results](prototype/README.md).

The reuse audit inspected exact upstream commits of Frictionless (MIT) and changedetection.io (Apache-2.0), including relevant code, configuration, releases, dependencies, tests and issues; upstream tests were not run. n8n received a license-only check and is source-available, with use/enterprise limitations. Public CSV competitor repositories with unclear licenses are alternatives to evaluate, not code cleared for copying. Models/datasets/assets and transitive dependencies are not covered by a root repository license. [Exact commits and findings](research/technical-audit.md).

Buying a modest hosted monitor can be more rational than maintaining browser workers; the observed changedetection hosted price was $8.99/month, subject to its actual limits/terms. A contractor's hours are a cash cost plus supervision, not autonomy. No GPU or local model is needed for the bounded prototype. A future production service would need persistent hosting, authenticated input, tenant isolation, durable queues/outbox, bounded retries, backups, restoration tests, cost caps and usable controls. None is implied by a successful local test.

Phone supervision uses existing mail, native scheduling and provider controls wherever adequate. The owner reviews a short weekly digest: booked sales versus settled cash, fee/refund/delivery estimates, contribution, queue age, failures, minutes and next decision. At two short checks plus one weekly batch, all reading/preview/taps count. A phone that displays an alert but cannot resolve it fails the gate. Documented Stripe native refunds do not prove the user's account works; Gumroad web refunds and actual mobile usability require testing. [Exact actions, sample digest and queue](plans/phone-operations.md).

Approved F3 downloads and scheduled Pins may continue during ten-hour absence; new drafts stay drafts. At 48 hours, unresolved paid-delivery problems take priority over growth. A week with little supervision exhausts scheduled content and may pause sales if support/refund queues exceed limits; this protects exposure but can lose revenue. F2/F4 approval-dependent delivery can miss commitments and require credits. Silence never authorizes new commitments. The operations appendix spells out continuation, pauses, deadlines and laptop recovery for every finalist.

## The single next experiment and stopping rule

Start with a four-hour original mockup and a fair comparison with the free/$19 substitutes. After separate launch/contact authorization, seek five relevant voluntary participants through the one chosen channel. If four prefer adequate existing solutions, stop. Only build a minimal product if a concrete simpler workflow is valued. Then seek at least 100 qualified offer-page visits and five unrelated $29 purchases over 6–8 weeks; zero sales at that denominator kills or revises the offer, and insufficient traffic means acquisition unresolved. Payment, delivery quality, repeated use, contribution and owner minutes are separate gates.

The later supervised pilot requires 30 paid orders and at least eight weeks before expanding routine automation; it still cannot prove rare-error safety or permanent unattended operation. Target <=120 minutes/week in two consecutive four-week periods, then test whether a <=60-minute tier can preserve outcomes. Keep the weekly editorial/commercial decision if it reduces errors or improves qualified conversion enough to repay its time. [Seven-day preparation, conditional 30-day implementation, sample content and explicit success/kill gates](plans/validation.md).

The ranking was materially revised after competitor discovery, fee-detail checks, mobile limitations and operator-workload failures. No earnings screenshots, follower count or GitHub popularity was treated as proof. Remaining uncertainties with the highest decision value are willingness to pay over adequate substitutes, achievable qualified traffic, actual support time, payment/refund access and owner competence. More generic idea searching is unlikely to resolve them. [Evidence ledger](research/evidence-ledger.md) and [checks/corrections](research/coverage-and-checks.md) distinguish completed checks from open ones.
