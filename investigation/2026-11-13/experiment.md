# One experiment, with measured stop rules

Prepared 13 November 2026. This plan starts its clock **after** the relevant external action is authorized and usable. Nothing has been sent, published, purchased or sold. Dollar amounts are USD planning values, not an exchange-rate quote. The maximum loss ceiling is $350; the first experiment should use much less.

## Hypothesis and primary uncertainty

An online gift shop that repeatedly answers customers in Hebrew and English or Russian will pay $149 for a policy-matched reply pack and use it in staff-assisted support. The pack must be worth more to that buyer than writing it internally or using existing AI/saved-reply tools. The central test is a real paid decision with a narrow scope—not likes, repository stars, the existence of an FAQ, or a compliment about the demo.

The owner must first demonstrate that they can judge both selected languages and recognize unsupported policy claims. If that fails, stop or narrow the service before acquiring customers. The package must not depend on a bot being deployed.

| 12–18 | First paid pilot if obtained; otherwise improve only a diagnosed acquisition problem | Delivery time and buyer acceptance, or an explicit distribution failure |

The prepared documents reduce blank-page work, but do not replace owner review or account/identity checks. Two pilots may need another 4–6 hours; do not force two orders into twenty hours. If registration/accounting is unresolved, continue interviews through an approved channel without accepting money. Model scenarios use their own explicit hours and are not a guarantee this schedule will be achieved.

## Acquisition sequence

1. **Primary proposal: one free Fiverr listing.** Request approval for account creation if needed and publication of the exact [offer](offer.md). The owner supplies truthful identity and handles identity verification. Publish only after scope/price/display are checked. No paid promotion or seller subscription. This is a low-cash exposure test, not a claim that a new account will receive traffic.
2. **Qualify inbound requests.** Ask what repeats, in which languages, how often, what the current workaround is, and what must be improved. Decline live-staffing, certified-translation, policy-writing or bot-installation jobs. Do not move platform customers off-platform.
3. **If exposure is weak, diagnose distribution before changing the product.** Twice weekly, search publicly visible buyer requests for the exact need. A matching request must be bounded writing/localization work with adequate approved source policies, not a job whose tag merely says FAQ. No matching ready buyers were established during research. Obtain separate authorization before applications, account creation or Connects spending. Proposed spending ceiling: $25 total, no boosting.
4. **Alternative only after explicit channel approval:** use the research invitation in a specific merchant community that permits it, or reply to an explicit request for this help. A public business website/contact form alone does not authorize cold marketing. The private four-business shortlist supplies qualification questions, not a bulk-send list. Do not infer that a one-time B2B message is legally exempt without checking the actual jurisdiction/channel.
5. **Never use urgency, invented case studies, anonymous customer records or promised performance.** Do not buy contact lists, scrape personal contacts or auto-send messages. No paid ads in this experiment.

This plan does not pretend a zero-audience listing reliably generates ten conversations. If it does not, the measured outcome is acquisition uncertainty. The next approved channel must have a concrete route to relevant people, not just a higher posting frequency.

## Ten-minute written interview

Ask about the buyer's actual workflow before showing the sample or defending the offer.

1. Which three questions did customers repeat last week? Approximately how often, in which languages, and who answered?
2. What do staff use now: memory, saved replies, an FAQ, a helpdesk, AI, or another person? Ask for an anonymized example only with permission.
3. What went wrong or consumed time? Ask for a recent incident rather than a hypothetical benefit.
4. Where does the answer already appear? Would moving a short answer near the purchase step help more than creating another FAQ?
5. Who can approve shipping, cancellation and exception wording? Are the policies clear enough to use as source material?
6. After showing the fictional sample: which part would you actually use, and which is unnecessary? What would a free tool or your current writer do equally well?
7. State the exact price and scope. “Would you like to book this $149 pilot, or is the existing solution sufficient?” A refusal is useful. Do not turn “interesting” into a sale.

Record the buyer's words, including “too expensive,” “no need,” “not enough trust,” “wrong languages,” and “need an operator, not documents.” Do not invent a loss reason when someone goes silent.

Re-check the business and whether the request is still active before any authorized response.

Track impressions, clicks, qualified conversations, offers, payments, approved deliveries, actual use and refunds separately. A freelancer competitor does not count as a buyer; a paid research participant's purchase is not independent willingness-to-pay evidence. Keep every denominator.

## Gates after 14 and 30 days

| Observation | Action |
|---|---|
| Language/policy review fails | Do not sell this package; narrow to a verified pair or assess fallback readiness |
| No impressions or qualified conversations after 14 days | Call it an acquisition failure so far. Check listing clarity/eligibility and one specific permitted alternative; do not buy ads |
| At least 10 qualified people see price/scope and none purchases | Stop this exact offer. Consider one substantial change only if refusal reasons support it; avoid endless discounts |
| People only want live support | Do not disguise staffing as a reply-pack win. Reassess schedule and a separately scoped service |
| 1 paid pilot | Deliver and learn; demand is still preliminary, no scale-up claim |
| 2 unrelated paying pilot buyers, both approve and use it, no invented promises, <=6 hours delivery each | Allow a second small batch, not a subscription/software launch |
| Delivery repeatedly >6 hours, repeated substantive revisions, or owner cannot judge accuracy | Pause sales; revise scope/price or stop. A willing buyer alone does not make delivery viable |
| 30 days or 12 acquisition hours with no paying buyer | Stop spending and active promotion of this attempt; diagnose whether exposure or offer failed. Consider the CSV fallback only after readiness, not automatic parallel launch |

For a second batch, aim for measured **at least $20/hour operating surplus before tax**, counting selling/admin/delivery time. This is an illustrative decision threshold, not the owner's stated wage requirement. Include initial setup when evaluating total project return; report both. If the owner requires $30/hour or rapid $1,000/month, the decision becomes stricter. Do not replace collected data with the optimistic scenario.

## Intake and delivery workflow

Before accepting an order, confirm the scope, selected languages, merchant approval contact, expected response window, complete-input date, platform terms and billing/tax treatment. Ask for current policy text/links, up to twenty anonymized recurring questions, tone preferences, prohibited promises and escalation rules. Do not request passwords, full inbox exports, payment information or real order records.

1. Create a policy fact table with the merchant. Flag conflicts, missing dates and undefined exception authority. Pause the affected wording until clarified.
2. Select twelve intents based on actual repetition. Draft both languages and five placement snippets. Each has source IDs, trigger, placeholders and “ask a person” conditions.
3. Review meaning and numerals across languages. Test a contradictory promise, a late delivery and a refund demand. A customer claim of a previous promise needs checking, not dismissal.
4. Deliver an editable document with use instructions. The merchant decides whether/where to install it. Do not silently change their store or enable automated answers.
5. Receive one consolidated revision request within seven calendar days of delivery; allow up to two business days for the included revision. Correct factual mistakes in the agreed work. New languages/policies/intents require a separately accepted scope; platform rules and mandatory rights still apply.
6. Ask, through the authorized customer channel, which replies were actually used after seven days and what required editing. Do not request a positive review or publish customer material without permission.

No guaranteed “minutes saved” claim. If the merchant chooses to measure, compare time on similar questions before/after and include the time spent searching/adapting replies. A handful of messages is preliminary and confounded, not a causal study.

## Absence and incident behavior

One active pilot. Check messages on disclosed working days; aim for one-business-day acknowledgment only if feasible. Ten hours away is compatible with document work when deadlines are agreed. Pause new orders before a planned multi-day absence. A missed deadline, policy error or refund dispute can add 3–6 owner hours; respond truthfully and follow the platform process. The service never promises to watch the merchant's live inbox. No automated external responses or scheduled spending are enabled.
