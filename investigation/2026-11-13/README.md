# Current decision: test a bilingual support-reply service

Decision date: **13 November 2026**, Israel time. Research conducted 12–13 November. This update supersedes the earlier craft-template recommendation; the earlier report remains an audit trail. **No paying demand has been validated.** No messages, listings, ads, purchases, account creation, or client work were executed. The repository push was explicitly requested.

Start with a **$149 fixed-price pilot** for a small online gift retailer: twelve reusable customer-service replies and five short FAQ/checkout snippets in **Hebrew plus English or Russian**, grounded in that merchant's approved policies. Deliver editable text and rules for when a human must check the order. Seven calendar days after complete inputs, one consolidated revision, one client at a time. This is a hypothesis worth testing, not a forecast of income.

The fallback is a **$99 tightly bounded CSV cleanup job**, using the owner's existing public automation examples after a readiness check. Do not launch both together. A hosted chatbot is a later possibility only after discovering a paying need and demonstrating operational competence.

## What changed with the owner's answers

Confirmed: at least twenty setup hours; willingness to spend time afterward; at most **$350 that can be lost**; phone-sales and customer-service experience; English, Russian and Hebrew; Israel; no existing customer access. Camera, voice, direct selling and contractor management are conditional preferences, not confirmed commitments. Language familiarity is not yet evidence of professional writing quality.

Unknown: desired income/deadline, sustainable weekly hours, payment/business-account status, technical troubleshooting ability, and comfort selling this particular offer. Use **6–10 owner hours/week during validation** as a planning allowance, not a promise. Keep ten-hour daytime absence as a conservative design condition, not a reported schedule. Use $300 and $1,000 monthly operating surplus only as illustrative milestones. The old <=60-minute weekly preference was provisional and should no longer drive the ranking.

The previous report correctly recognized competition and distinguished a prototype from a business. Its $29 template was selected mainly for low ongoing attention. That advantage no longer outweighs the lack of craft expertise or audience. Its assumed acquisition cost is particularly weak: scheduled content production is not the same as acquiring buyers. The newer calculation includes research, selling, unanswered conversations, revisions and setup hours.

During the final repository sync, [a separate income report](../../research/claude-income.md) arrived through an already-merged upstream change. It favors selling existing chat-software setups and assumes a reserved project budget, 8–12 weekly hours and a two-month income goal. Those assumptions were not confirmed in this chat. It is preserved unchanged as a competing recommendation, not silently adopted or overwritten. Its vendor price examples and untested conversion estimates do not establish sales. A narrow website-chat-only setup could be feasible before optional messaging integrations; pending optional integrations alone are not a reason to reject the core product. The decisive missing evidence here is successful live acceptance, a competent support owner and a buyer willing to pay. If those are established or the owner prioritizes that project, the ranking should change. This update does not revalidate every legal, model-pricing or program claim in that separate report.

## Ranking and sensitivity

Hard constraints come first: $350 maximum loss, credible delivery, no dependence on a response during the job, and no unapproved external action. Then favor existing skills/assets, low cost to reach a meaningful buying decision, and bounded support. Ordinal ranks express judgment, not calculated success probabilities.

| Rank | Candidate | Owner fit | Acquisition uncertainty | Delivery/incident risk | Why this position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bilingual support replies | Closest to known experience; writing quality untested | High | Moderate, bounded by approved policies | Can test a specific paid outcome without building software |
| 2 | CSV cleanup | Existing tested examples; operator competence untested | High; commodity competition | Moderate/high if rules wrong | Concrete portfolio already exists, but more technical responsibility |
| 3 | Original searchable tutorials leading to one of those services | Topics can come from real work | Very high from zero audience | Low urgent support; high production burden | Adds reusable evidence after delivery competence |
| 4 | Craft closeout workbook | No demonstrated craft-market access | Very high; substitutes cheap | Low/moderate | Low maintenance cannot compensate for weak reason to switch |
| 5 | Hosted chatbot | Potential reusable software asset | High; native tools compete | Highest: incidents and customer data | Too many simultaneous uncertainties for first $350 |

With only one hour/week afterward, none is established as a reliable income source; the static workbook rises operationally, but revenue may remain zero. With 10–15 hours/week and comfort selling, the two services rise. With a genuine craft audience, the workbook rises sharply. With proven coding/support capability and a paying design partner, a chatbot can rise above content. More capital alone does not fix customer acquisition: even $2,000 should not fund ads or contractors before contribution and delivery are measured. If the owner will not sell directly, marketplace/inbound content tests remain possible but slower and less controllable. If the objective is urgent dependable income, a suitable paid support role may be more rational than a business experiment; current sample listings often require 30–41 hours/week [S38].

## 1. Bilingual reply pack: first experiment

**Buyer and job.** An owner-operated gift shop serving customers in two of the owner's languages, receiving recurring delivery, personalization and order-change questions. The purchaser wants staff to answer consistently without rewriting policy every time. Require at least two hours/week of relevant repeated work or another concrete costly mistake; a shop with five easy questions a month is unlikely to justify $149. The public-site shortlist contains four research candidates, not four customers, and is kept in local private notes to avoid publishing speculative business assessments.

**Evidence and rejection case.** An anonymous ecommerce operator reports repetitive questions despite an FAQ [S05]. This is pain evidence, not proof of a purchase. Shopify already provides saved replies and editable policy-based answers [S06–S07]. Its documented automatic instant-answer suggestions are English-only; that specific limitation is a possible angle, not a claim that all Shopify AI or translation tools lack Hebrew/Russian. Fiverr already lists FAQ/reply-writing offers at low asking prices [S08]. Our edge, if any, is policy matching, idiomatic review and an easy handoff—not the existence of text generation. Buyers may prefer their own staff, existing AI or a cheaper writer.

**Deliverable and acquisition.** See the completed offer and original synthetic sample. First propose one free marketplace listing with the sample; no ads. After authorization, inspect buyer requests twice weekly and respond only to requests genuinely matching the package, within an approved application budget. The public research did **not** locate ten ready buyers for this exact two-language package. A search category count and a merchant contact form are not demand. A listing with no exposure tests distribution poorly; it cannot reject the service. A paid sample followed by use in real work is stronger evidence than survey enthusiasm.

**Division of work.** AI can cluster anonymized questions, draft alternatives, compare each reply to a supplied policy and flag inconsistent promises. The owner must judge language/tone, choose facts, resolve ambiguity with the merchant, check every reply and sell the work. The merchant approves policy and refund/escalation authority. No certification, legal drafting, automatic replies, inbox access or guaranteed ticket reduction is claimed. Do not reuse employer materials or customer records. A reviewer is optional only after separately approved spending and data permission.

**Economics and time.** At $149, a direct-sale planning case leaves $111.15 contribution before fixed costs, owner labor and tax. It reserves 10% + $0.50 for collection/FX, 5% for expected refunds, $5 for delivery tools and $10 cash acquisition per order. These are assumptions, not a processor quote. Two pilots leave $207.30 after $15 fixed cost; at fifteen operating hours, that is $13.82/hour before tax and before six setup hours. Including setup reduces it to $9.87/hour. It is learning income, not yet an attractive established business. Four jobs under improved delivery/sales assumptions leave $429.60 over eighteen operating hours ($23.87/hour before tax); those efficiencies must be earned. More cases and $1,000 workload appear in the model.

**Cheapest meaningful test.** Owner reviews the multilingual sample; obtain ten qualified price discussions after permitted acquisition, aiming for two paid $149 pilots. Track all sales hours and reasons for refusal. Advance only if both customers approve/use the work, no policy promises are invented, delivery fits the cap, and recurring economics are acceptable. Ten price discussions is a small operational gate, not statistical proof. Full decision rules are in the experiment file.

## 2. CSV cleanup: fallback with existing evidence

**Buyer/outcome.** A small merchant with one messy stock export wants an agreed normalized output plus a transparent rejected-row report, not a general-purpose app. Start with one CSV, <=1,000 rows and <=20 columns, one output schema, no sensitive personal data, and no store import. Price hypothesis: $99. More files, APIs or uncertain rules need a new scope, not an unlimited revision.

**Assets and contrary evidence.** The public [automation-samples repository](https://github.com/StepenkoAnatoli/automation-samples) contains real before/after examples. At reviewed commit `fccf9007055764fcc4ec6a41b177643d2b71b5b7`, the CSV tests passed **49/49**, and the API-to-CSV tests passed **18/18** locally. This supports the documented fixture behavior, not production reliability, the owner's debugging ability, or paid demand. Competing cleaning offers start around $20–$50 [S09]. A $100 buyer post found in research included additional NLP/API work and heavy competition; it was rejected as a matching lead [S10].

**Human/AI split and risk.** AI can propose deterministic mappings and explain rejected rows. The owner must approve identifier namespace, decimal/date interpretation, currency handling and duplicate rules with the buyer. The existing example's default day-first dates, assumed ILS for missing currency and newest-row duplicate rule are not universally correct. No unattended production writes. The readiness exercise in the fallback file requires explaining those exact pitfalls before accepting money.

**Economics and test.** $99 under the same conservative direct-sale reserves leaves $68.65 contribution. At five all-in hours, that is $13.73/hour before fixed costs/tax. Test one paid, bounded job after the owner can audit the sample. Continue only if total delivery <=3 hours, no unexplained deletions, and a buyer explicitly prefers the result to a free/cheap alternative. API automation remains outside the first package. A data-interpretation incident may take a full evening or require a refund.

## 3. Original searchable tutorials: audience building with an offer

**Audience/offer.** Merchants looking for a concrete task such as distinguishing dispatch from delivery in saved replies, or reviewing duplicate SKUs safely. Publish original screen demonstrations using synthetic examples; the call to action is the fixed service. This can work without a face, but narration willingness and clear explanation still matter. Reviews require actually using the product; do not synthesize fake hands-on experience. Affiliate disclosure and platform eligibility need checking before use.

**Why not ads first.** Full YouTube advertising eligibility ordinarily requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualifying public watch hours in twelve months, or 10 million qualifying Shorts views in ninety days, plus the other program requirements [S11]. Earlier access to some YPP features is not equivalent to full ad revenue. Originality rules make mass-produced repetitive content a poor shortcut [S12]. One operator's burnout account illustrates possible high effort and disappointing returns, not the typical outcome [S13].

**Work and AI.** Budget 20–36 hours for four useful 5–8 minute videos: choose one task, verify the procedure, record, edit, thumbnail/title, captions and distribution. AI helps outlines, captions and editing suggestions; the owner must demonstrate and check the procedure. Shorts may reuse a coherent excerpt, with more time for editing/comments. Two additional hours/week for distribution and analytics is a test allowance, not optional hidden labor. Use existing recording equipment; initial spend $0–$30 if authorized, with no purchase assumed.

**Economics and gate.** At an illustrative $5 net ad RPM, $1,000 requires 200,000 monetized views; $5 is a sensitivity input, not an Israel/channel forecast. At illustrative 1% link click and 2% click-to-purchase conversion, 10,000 relevant views yield two service orders before capacity/refunds. All three rates may be zero. Four videos with no qualified inquiry justify revising topic/offer/distribution, not buying more automation. Only advance once delivery competence exists; doing this simultaneously with the first service would spread twenty hours too thin.

## 4. Craft closeout workbook: preserve the useful test, demote the launch

**Buyer/outcome.** A craft seller comparing event profitability and inventory. The old $29 proposal remains plausible only if it meaningfully simplifies a specific workflow. A $19 competitor, a free tracker and software free tiers already exist [S01–S04]. One old customer discussion and a newer event-tracking discussion show diverse workarounds, not consensus demand for a new workbook [S02, S23]. Existing spreadsheet users can have substantial switching friction.

**Distribution and workload.** Pinterest search is a hypothesis; two weekly Pins can still produce no traffic. Allow 12–20 initial hours for an original, checked workbook, 2–4 growth hours/week, and 0.5–2 routine hours/week only after support stabilizes; an incompatible workbook can consume several extra hours. Native Pinterest scheduling helps batching but does not create demand [S16]. AI drafts instructions and formulas; the owner or qualified reviewer must verify every financial example and compatibility claim.

**Economics/test.** At $29, modeled Gumroad direct collection fees, 5% refund allowance and $6 cash acquisition leave $16.01 before fixed costs and labor. Twenty sales leave $305.18 after $15 fixed cost; with sixteen recurring hours that is $19.07/hour before tax, and much less if acquisition takes forty hours. Gumroad Discover has a different 30% fee [S27]. First compare a proposed workflow against the existing free/$19 choices with five consenting relevant users. If they cannot identify a worthwhile difference, stop before building. Revenue from repeated use of one purchase is not recurring revenue.

## 5. Hosted chatbot: technical assets do not resolve business risk

**Buyer/outcome.** A merchant may pay for reliable answers and fewer repetitive contacts. The buyer could instead configure native quick replies, instant answers or an existing helpdesk. Selling a hosted bot means selling operational reliability, policy maintenance and escalation as well as code [S06–S07]. This category must be evaluated independently of the pleasure of building it.

**Delivery and exposure.** Plan for authenticated administration, tenant separation, data retention, consent and permissions, provider costs/rate limits, misleading answers, model/provider changes, backups and incident handling. Exact integrations and their acceptance tests must be proven before promising them. AI can help tests and configuration; a technically competent human must own deployment, access control and incident judgment. No reviewed repository is being represented as a security-audited, live-verified commercial service.

**Economics/test.** A hypothetical $49 subscription minus $15 infrastructure/usage and $5 collection allowance leaves $29 before support, acquisition, refunds and taxes. One hour of monthly support valued at $30 already removes the margin; setup is additional. These inputs are assumptions, not a quote for any particular implementation. Twenty setup hours and $350 cannot be treated as sufficient for an unknown production deployment. First learn from a paid reply-pack customer whether a specific manual step deserves automation. Require a named paying design partner, explicit boundaries and successful live acceptance tests before a separately budgeted pilot. Ten-hour absence is tolerable for document delivery; it may be unacceptable for a failed live integration.

## Cash, operating limits and what would change the decision

Israel-specific payment eligibility is a launch dependency, not a reason to incorporate abroad. PayPal publishes Israeli business fees, but an account's approval, holds, currency costs and payout path remain unverified [S28]. Stripe's checked supported-country list did not list Israel [S29]. Lemon Squeezy's prohibited-products page excludes services; do not disguise a service as a software download [S30]. Fiverr retains 20% and normally has a clearing period [S31]. Upwork's contract-specific fee and Connects costs mean proposals are not automatically free [S32–S33]. Platform fees, FX, refunds, taxes and owner labor are distinct costs.

The government provides an online business-registration route, but eligibility and the owner's precise tax/invoicing obligations require checking before taking payment [S34]. The calculations exclude income tax and any output VAT liability; if the quoted customer price must include VAT, recompute the net price. No tax exemption is inferred from a small pilot. Registration/accounting costs have not been quoted.

The $350 is a maximum loss ceiling, not a spending target. Proposed allocations: $25 acquisition, $25 tools, $60 optional language review, $40 administration, $150 refund/dispute liquidity, $50 unallocated. None is spent or authorized merely by this plan. Keep customer prepayments available against unearned work, separate from this allowance; expected refund percentages do not fund a whole refund. Start with $0 reversible preparation. Reassess if required administration exceeds the allowance rather than quietly expanding it.

Pause new orders during an absence. Promise one-business-day acknowledgment only when it can be met, seven-calendar-day delivery after complete inputs, and no emergency/live support. One active pilot limits overload. A normal first-delivery week may take 6–10 owner hours including selling; revisions or a dispute can add 3–6 hours. An extended absence requires pausing sales and communicating existing deadlines through authorized channels.

The next uncertainty is **whether a qualified buyer will pay for this exact package and use it**, followed by whether the owner can deliver accurately and profitably. More software or a larger general-purpose research report would not answer that. The prepared offer, sample and decision gates are ready for review. External testing still needs the specific authorization required by the original request.
