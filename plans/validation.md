# One first experiment: a craft-market closeout template

**Selected experiment: F3 / S05 / N03.** This is a test of a paid outcome and acquisition channel, not an instruction to launch several businesses. The catalog-preflight, monitoring, creator-tool and service tests elsewhere are fallback research designs. No customer contact, posting, account creation, purchase, advertising or collection of money has been executed or authorized by this report. The requested GitHub push publishes this research package only.

## Exact audience, channel, and offer

Audience: solo handmade-product sellers who attend multiple physical craft markets and currently use paper or a general spreadsheet to compare events. Primary acquisition channel: **Pinterest**, using two original, useful static Pins per week. Offer hypothesis: **$29 once** for one original, narrow event-closeout and event-comparison workbook, sample copy and short captioned walkthrough. One supported spreadsheet platform in the first version, no macros, login integrations, custom formulas, live stock synchronization, bookkeeping or tax advice. Support is asynchronous within two business days, with support days disclosed. The customer may use the workbook repeatedly; that does not make revenue recurring. No subscription is proposed.

The observed problem is real enough to test: a craft seller asks how to compare inventory across markets while already using Paper and Spark. Other respondents use paper, Excel or inventory software. [Original discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/CraftFairs/comments/1givbp2/tracking_inventory/). The rejection case is strong: a competitor already offers event comparison, inventory reconciliation and planning for $19, with a $29 bundle. [Plainsheet offer](https://plainsheetstudio.com/makers/templates/craft-fair-tracker/). A free alternative also exists. [Sparks of Joy tracker](https://www.sparksofjoystudio.com/digital/p/craft-fair-inventory-sales-tracker). These sources prove available substitutes and reported pain, not that a new seller can charge $29.

The only proposed switching advantage is **less setup and a clearer five-minute end-of-event decision** for this particular buyer. This has not been demonstrated, and competitors may already be easier. Do not copy their formulas, artwork, instructions or sample data. The product has no proven moat. If the owner cannot explain and independently check its calculations, the experiment stops or uses a priced qualified reviewer; AI output is not expertise.

## Stage 0 — falsify the difference before building

Prepare one original, nonfunctional mockup with synthetic data and a one-paragraph offer; cap preparation at four laptop hours. Show side-by-side descriptions of the free/$19 alternatives and our proposed $29 version to five relevant people who voluntarily opt in after future authorized publication. Do not solicit in communities that prohibit promotion, and do not count friends or paid testers as demand. Usability testers may be compensated later, but those payments are research costs and their own purchases do not count as willingness-to-pay evidence.

Ask neutral questions: “How did you decide which event to repeat last time?”, “What does your current method miss?”, “Which of these options would you use and why?”, “What would make the $29 option unnecessary?” Do not lead with AI or promise profit. Record the current workaround, last real event, time spent, software used, preferred alternative and reason. If at least four of five say existing/free tools solve the task, stop before a workbook build. If fewer than three identify a concrete unresolved task and prefer the simpler proposed workflow, revise once or stop. This small convenience sample cannot estimate market size or conversion; it can reveal a fatal lack of difference.

Recruitment is not assumed available: a new Pinterest account may not produce five qualified volunteers quickly. If opt-ins fail, label **acquisition unresolved**, not “buyers rejected the product.” No second channel is added during this test to rescue a flattering result. A later change of channel is a new experiment with its own time/cost budget.

## Stage 1 — actual payment, not likes

Only after Stage 0 and account/quality gates pass, create a minimal complete product, plain offer page and a free one-event example. The paid file must work before taking non-refundable payment. Publish two approved Pins weekly for **6–8 weeks**. A 7-day sprint is preparation; even 30 days may produce too little organic reach. Cap the test at eight weeks and the declared budget; a deliberate extension requires a new decision, not silent spending.

Define a qualified visit before launch: a non-bot visit arriving from the intended market/Pinterest content to the actual offer page with the price and compatibility visible, excluding owner's/testing traffic. Report outbound clicks, landing visits and unique buyers separately; do not equate platform impressions with people or buyers. Use consent-appropriate, minimal analytics; no tracking workaround or invented attribution.

Predeclared gate: at least **100 qualified offer-page visits and five unrelated paid $29 orders** within the observation window, no severe formula error, refunds <=10% of orders, observed median setup <=15 minutes in voluntary buyer tests, support <=10 minutes/order on average, and no need for workday emergency responses. Five sales is a signal only. Under the illustrative 2% conversion assumption, 100 visits would yield two expected orders, so the five-payment gate is deliberately stronger than that base assumption; if there are two to four payments, mark mixed evidence and allow at most one scoped follow-up rather than declaring success.

Kill/revise once if 100 qualified visits produce zero sales, most opt-ins prefer the free alternative, or buyers need customized integrations. Stop immediately for a severe arithmetic/rights defect until fixed and affected buyers handled. If eight weeks yields fewer than 100 visits, acquisition remains unproven; do not spend the rest of a $5,000 budget to force the denominator. Record refunds separately from orders: five purchases followed by five refunds is not success.

Zero sales in 100 independent qualified visits gives an approximate 95% upper bound near 3% for conversion under a simple binomial model; visits are not guaranteed independent or representative, so even this is only a rough diagnostic. Five payments do not establish retention, scalable CAC, rare-error safety or a sustainable 60-minute week.

## Stage 2 — supervised paid pilot

After Stage 1, observe **30 paid orders and at least eight weeks**, whichever takes longer, with a maximum 12-week pilot before explicit reassessment. This may exceed the 30-day implementation plan. If orders are slow, report that; do not manufacture purchases or merge audiences. Track the same customer/order IDs through visits where consent permits, purchase, successful download, setup, support, refund and later use. Record actual cash received and all expenses, including samples/review fees, failed assets and rework.

Measure repeat usage at a second event for at least five consenting buyers within 60 days of purchase; a one-off digital product does not have subscription churn. If seasonality prevents a second event, repeat-use evidence remains unknown. Repeat purchases are neither required nor presumed. No upsell, affiliate revenue or sponsorship is counted in the selected model.

Owner-minute log categories: content research, making/previewing assets, scheduling, acquisition/analytics, quality/version review, reading notifications, support, refunds, fixes, accounting, platform maintenance and incidents. Start/stop a timer; batch work still counts. Buyers' time is measured separately from owner's time. A 30-order sample can estimate common support friction but cannot establish low rare-error rates. Even zero incidents in 30 orders permits a rough upper bound of ~10% under simple assumptions; preserve review and compatibility limits.

## Automation graduation rules

| Gate | Minimum evidence | Permitted next automation | Failure response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product quality | All seeded arithmetic/edge cases pass; no severe defect in 30 paid deliveries; experienced reviewer checks model logic | Deliver the exact reviewed immutable version automatically | Stop sales of defective version, preserve existing access if safe, contact/refund affected buyers through authorized process |
| Delivery reliability | <5% owner-handled delivery failures, receipts/export reconciled, recovery drill completed | Routine paid-download delivery and receipt resend through supported platform | Return to manual batch delivery or pause sales; do not keep charging during broken fulfillment |
| Owner workload | Two consecutive four-week periods <=120 min/week including acquisition/admin; target <=60 only after measured reduction | Increase pre-approved content queue modestly within native limits | Narrow scope/cadence; refuse custom work; stop if persistent overload |
| Mobile recovery | Actual owner phone can preview, pause offer/content, resolve one support case, issue/refund through eligible route, and confirm outcome | Routine phone supervision | No “phone-operated” claim; retain explicit laptop path or choose a different provider |
| Unit economics | Positive measured contribution after fees/refunds/CAC; credible full cost ledger | Reinvest only a predefined part of settled cash | Stop paid acquisition when marginal contribution <=0 |
| Boundaries | No same-day workday obligation; asset rights, payment eligibility and required platform confirmations satisfied | Approved routine only | Hold new commitments; silence never approves |

A failure does not authorize bypassing a platform confirmation or reducing disclosure. Expansion is conditional, not scheduled inevitability. The owner keeps the useful weekly decision: choose which customer problem the next two original Pins explain and whether any product/support trend warrants a fix.

These are ceilings, not spending instructions or vendor quotes. Stage 0 ideally costs $0 cash; all reviewer/ad spending requires later authorization. If a competent review cannot be obtained within the allocation and owner cannot check the work, stop rather than skip quality. Use native scheduling/free commerce tier if eligible. Do not buy annual subscriptions. Refund liquidity must be accessible in the relevant processor, not merely in a bank. Some providers impose minimums/holds; test that path before launch. Initial losses may consume the operating spend, and refunds consume liquidity; these are not legal limits on liability. Owner setup time 12–24 hours at $30/h adds $360–720 economic cost before launch; recurring production is separate. Zero revenue for two months is an explicit planning case.

At the base modeled 20 orders/month, $580 gross produces ~$329 operating profit before owner pay/tax and ~$155 after $30/hour owner labor, conditional on $6 cash CAC and ~80 weekly minutes. This is not the pilot forecast. If paid visits cost $0.50 and convert at 2%, CAC is $25: the $29 product loses money at the margin. Organic Pinterest does not eliminate acquisition work; the base has 75 scheduled minutes/week plus exceptions. To stay below 60 minutes, reduce cadence or scope and accept slower acquisition; do not assume the same sales with less work.

The cash table funds the initial eight-week demand test. A later 8–12-week operational pilot is a separate gate: allow at most another $45 of fixed costs, funded only from settled surplus after refund obligations or a separately authorized allocation. The table is not a promise that all later months fit the same $30 operating provision. No gate automatically authorizes expenditure above the initial cap.

## Thirty-day conditional implementation plan

Days 1–7 are the preparation sprint above. Days 8–14, **after publication/contact authorization**, begin the single-channel mockup/opt-in test; keep the product unbuilt until Stage 0 suggests a useful distinction. If five relevant opt-ins do not arrive, continue observing within the eight-week cap; the calendar does not override the gate. Days 15–21, only if the distinction gate passes, build the narrow workbook (6–10 laptop hours), original instructions and synthetic cases; test the one supported platform, blanks/zeros/returns/damage/fees, repeated events and totals. Obtain qualified usability/arithmetic review. Do not add live integrations.

Days 22–30, only after quality and account gates pass, complete a permitted test purchase/refund/download, check the actual phone and scheduler, publish the reviewed offer and two original Pins/week, and begin paid observation. A real paid pilot may start after day 30. If the experiment has not earned a gate, the deliverable is the measured failure/uncertainty, not a hurried launch. Continued weeks 5–8 measure acquisition and payments; the 30-order operational pilot extends further if warranted.

One original sample brief: **“Two $400 market days can leave very different money for you.”** Use clearly synthetic examples: Market A $400 sales minus $120 goods, $90 booth, $30 travel and $12 card fees leaves $148 before owner pay/tax; Market B $400 minus $120, $40, $10 and $12 leaves $218. Put both in one readable static Pin, show the assumptions, and link to the one-event sample. The product teaches a repeatable comparison; it does not recommend investments or claim the buyer will earn more. No music, borrowed creator footage or fabricated product experience is needed.

Phone review for two Pins: inspect numbers and claim (3 min), destination/preview (2 min), native scheduling (2–3 min), confirm queue (1 min). Estimated 8–9 minutes is included in the scheduled 75, not an extra hidden activity. Faceless static is the default. A captioned screen demo adds roughly 15–30 production minutes; voice-led adds 15–30; on-camera adds 30–60 plus willingness/rights considerations. Add one only if it measurably improves qualified conversion enough to justify the burden.

Alternative finalists' first-month plans, **not concurrent projects**: F1 spends week 1 comparing free tools, week 2 on voluntary agency task evidence, week 3 on at most three scoped paid reports and week 4 on repeat-use evidence; hosted build requires later payment gates and another 35–60h. F2 prepares two source-linked briefs, verifies source rights/coverage, then tests five buyers and one paid issue before building scheduling; 10–20h setup and weekly editorial time remain. F4 validates one exact export/report with two consenting buyers, fixes the data contract, delivers two paid monthly cycles and only then automates; 30 days cannot demonstrate repeat demand for two monthly cycles. These plans are retained for comparison only.
